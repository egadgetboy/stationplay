**Fixes: a converted copy when a player can't decode a file, no file names in the apps, problems from the apps with more to go on, and a copy of the database before every update.**

- **A converted copy when a player can't decode a file.** When a device's player can't decode a file (as on some Samsung phones), StationPlay's apps can now ask for a converted copy instead of trying the same thing again: an ordinary H.264 picture at up to 1080p, with AAC sound (5.1 where the device takes surround sound), from where the viewer was, with the same version, sound, subtitles and night mode, and within the quality you allow away from home. It counts toward the copies converted at once, and the log says the app asked for it, and what went wrong just before. Also: a wide film converted at 1080p is now 1920 pixels wide, as 1080p players take it (it was wider), and devices asking at the same moment can no longer all get past the limit on copies converted at once.
- **No file names in the apps.** Nothing StationPlay's apps receive names a file or a folder. A sound or subtitle track whose title in the file is a file's name is listed without it, and the alert about a data folder that can't be written to no longer gives its path. The page and the log still name files, for Admins.
- **Problems in the apps, with more to go on.** On the **Logs** tab, each problem now says **What it means** in plain words for the errors StationPlay knows (a player that couldn't decode a file, a format the device doesn't play, a lost connection, and more), then **What the app said**, and **What led up to it** shows the app's last lines before it. An app that can't reach StationPlay for a minute or more says so once it's back ("Den couldn't reach StationPlay for 12 minutes · No network on this device"), and sends what went wrong meanwhile, each at the time it happened.
- **"Subtitles", one word.** What StationPlay gives the apps to show, and its documents, say subtitles where they said captions, as the apps do.
- **A copy of the database before every update.** From now on, when an update needs to change StationPlay's database, StationPlay first copies the database as it is (sign-ins included) to `data/backups` as `before-<version>-<date>.db`, says so on the **Logs** tab, and only then changes it. It keeps the newest 3. If it can't make the copy (its disk is full, say), it changes nothing, says why in its log, and stops. 1.29.1 is such an update.
- **In the apps.** Asking for a converted copy, the problems' journals and telling StationPlay about trouble reaching it come with the apps' own updates (StationPlay for Android 0.3.1).

**Files.** `stationplay-1.29.1.zip` is the app (it unzips to `src`); `stationplay.yaml` is the app definition for TrueNAS SCALE, and `docker-compose.yml` for everything else.

**Updating.** Unzip `stationplay-1.29.1.zip` over `src` first, then change only the version in `image:` to `stationplay:1.29.1` (see [Updating](https://github.com/egadgetboy/stationplay#updating)). Your stations, settings and sign-ins are kept, and so is your Plex token. As it starts, 1.29.1 saves a copy of the database in `data/backups` as `before-1.29.1-<date>.db`, then updates it. **New install:** see [Install](https://github.com/egadgetboy/stationplay#install).

**Rolling back.** To go back to 1.29.0 (see [Rolling back](https://github.com/egadgetboy/stationplay#rolling-back)):

1. Stop StationPlay.
2. In the data folder, replace `stationplay.db` with `backups/before-1.29.1-<date>.db`, and delete `stationplay.db-wal` and `stationplay.db-shm` if they're there.
3. Unzip `stationplay-1.29.0.zip` over `src`.
4. Change `image:` back to `stationplay:1.29.0`, and start it.

Everyone stays signed in, and everything is as it was just before the update; anything changed since is lost.
